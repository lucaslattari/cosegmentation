\subsection{Calibration Protocol}
\label{sec:ablation-setup}

Running the full grid over all 38 iCoseg classes for every stage would
be computationally prohibitive, particularly for the multi-axis sweeps
of Sec.~\ref{sec:ablation}. All ablation and calibration decisions
reported in this paper are therefore conducted on a fixed set of 14
curated classes, selected to span the range of object types present in
iCoseg --- static rigid landmarks (Stonehenge, Taj Mahal, Statue,
Ferrari), articulated animals (Alaskan Brown Bear, Brown Bear, Elephant,
Panda), multi-instance crowd scenes (Red Sox Players, Liverpool,
Gymnastics, Skating), and non-rigid deformable objects (Balloon, Kite)
--- rather than an exhaustive or statistically stratified sample.
Hyperparameter decisions are accepted only when they improve both the
mean performance across these 14 classes and do not catastrophically
degrade any individual class (Sec.~\ref{sec:results}), consistent with
our generalization-over-per-class-optimization principle; per-class
parameter overrides are treated as overfitting and rejected regardless
of the local gain they would produce.

We note that these 14 calibration classes are a subset of the 38
iCoseg classes used for the honest end-to-end evaluation of
Sec.~\ref{sec:results} (the icoseg38 protocol): hyperparameter
selection throughout this section is therefore supervised by ground
truth on part of the evaluation set, not performed on a fully
disjoint validation split. Inference itself (Stages E1--E5) requires
no ground truth and no gradient-based training. Where computationally
feasible, we quantify sensitivity to this overlap via leave-one-class-out
re-selection (Sec.~\ref{sec:ablation-e4}); extending this check to every
sweep in this section is left to future work.

\subsection{Stage~E1 Diagnostic Score}
\label{sec:e1-diagnostics}

Because the extracted features of Sec.~\ref{sec:e1} are not by
themselves a segmentation, evaluating a candidate E1 configuration
directly against ground truth would require running the full E1--E5
pipeline for every grid point, which is intractable at the scale of the
sweep in Sec.~\ref{sec:ablation}. We instead score each configuration
with an unsupervised diagnostic, \texttt{proxy\_score}, computed
directly from the cached descriptors of a collection without ground
truth or any downstream stage:
\begin{equation}
\texttt{proxy\_score} = 0.55\,\bar{s}_{\text{nn}} + 0.35\,\bar{s}_{\text{cls}}
  - 0.10\,\sigma_{\text{nn}} - 0.05\,\text{CV}_{\text{norm}},
\end{equation}
where $\bar{s}_{\text{nn}}$ is the mean, over all image pairs in the
collection, of a symmetric nearest-neighbour patch similarity (a
subsample of up to 128 $\ell_2$-normalised patch descriptors per image,
scored by the mean best-match cosine similarity in both directions);
$\bar{s}_{\text{cls}}$ is the mean pairwise cosine similarity between
$\ell_2$-normalised class tokens; $\sigma_{\text{nn}}$ is the
standard deviation of the pairwise nearest-neighbour score across all
pairs, penalising configurations whose correspondence quality is
inconsistent rather than merely low on average; and
$\text{CV}_{\text{norm}}$ is the coefficient of variation of raw patch
descriptor norms within the collection. The first two terms approximate,
without running Stage~E2, how well patch- and image-level correspondence
would behave under a given configuration; the last two penalise
inconsistency and non-uniform activation magnitude.
\texttt{proxy\_score} is therefore a proxy for correspondence quality,
not a proxy for final segmentation accuracy, and Sec.~\ref{sec:results}
confirms that intermediate-stage metrics of this kind do not reliably
predict end-to-end results --- it is used here strictly for coarse
triage over a large configuration grid, not as a substitute for the
honest, ground-truth-based evaluation reported elsewhere in this paper.
We note, for transparency, that $\text{CV}_{\text{norm}}$ is
identically zero under $\ell_2$-normalised features (\texttt{l2}) by
construction and strictly positive under \texttt{raw} features,
introducing a small (5\% weight) built-in preference for \texttt{l2}
independent of any property of the descriptors themselves; this
accounts for the small, consistent margin by which \texttt{l2}
configurations outrank their otherwise-identical \texttt{raw}
counterparts in Table~\ref{tab:e1-sweep-top10}.

Table~\ref{tab:e1-sweep-top10} reports the ten highest-ranked configurations
from a grid sweep over backbone (\texttt{vits14}, \texttt{vitb14},
\texttt{vitl14}), input size ($S \in \{448, 518, 672\}$), resize mode, and
feature post-processing, evaluated on the 14 curated calibration classes
(Sec.~\ref{sec:ablation-setup}) via the unsupervised diagnostic
\texttt{proxy\_score} of Sec.~\ref{sec:e1-diagnostics}. The six
highest-ranked configurations differ by at most $0.0057$ in mean
\texttt{proxy\_score} --- less than one-tenth of the per-configuration
standard deviation ($\text{std} \approx 0.07$) --- and are therefore not
statistically distinguishable from one another at the resolution of this
sweep: the aggregate metric alone does not select between
$S \in \{448, 518, 672\}$ or between \texttt{l2} and \texttt{raw}
post-processing. Our production configuration ($S=518$,
\texttt{keep\_ar}, \texttt{l2}) falls within this indistinguishable
group (rank 2), consistent with the geometric argument of
Sec.~\ref{sec:e1} rather than an empirical claim of superiority; the
sweep's role here is to confirm the absence of a systematically better
alternative within the grid, not to justify the choice on its own.

We retain \texttt{vits14+l2} as the production default: it leads the
aggregate ranking, wins a plurality of individual classes, and incurs
substantially lower computational cost than \texttt{vitl14}, at

Sec.~\ref{sec:ablation-setup}. We revisit this trade-off, together with

\subsection{Stage~E2 Calibration: Correspondence Pair Count}
\label{sec:ablation-e2}

Stage E2's production hyperparameters ($K$ pairs, temperature $\tau$,
degree-reweighting $\alpha$, orientation heuristic) were originally
calibrated on the same 6-class subset used for Stages~E1 and~E3. We
recalibrated $K$ and $\tau$ on the full 14-class set of
Sec.~\ref{sec:ablation-setup}, using a diagnostic analogous to
Sec.~\ref{sec:e1-diagnostics}: an unsupervised \texttt{proxy\_score},
computed directly from a Stage~E2 output (\texttt{correspondence.npz})
without any downstream stage, combining foreground-score polarization,
pairwise vote agreement, leave-one-out stability, and correlation with
the class-token saliency map. Ground-truth-based diagnostics
(\texttt{gt\_best\_iou}, an oracle-thresholded IoU on the raw
foreground scores, and \texttt{orientation\_accuracy}) are also
reported, computed the same way as in Sec.~\ref{sec:e1-diagnostics}.

Across the full grid ($K \in \{20,\dots,400\}$, $\tau \in
\{0.02,0.05,0.10,0.20\}$, all 14 classes, 100\% coverage),
$\tau=0.05$ yields the highest mean \texttt{gt\_best\_iou} at every
single value of $K$ tested, with no exception --- a stronger and more
uniform result than the corresponding comparison in Stage~E1. As in
Sec.~\ref{sec:e1-diagnostics}, however, the unsupervised
\texttt{proxy\_score} disagrees with this ranking: its top-ranked
configuration is $(K, \tau) = (80, 0.10)$, whose \texttt{gt\_best\_iou}
($0.438$) is substantially below what $\tau=0.05$ achieves at any
$K \geq 150$. We again report this discrepancy rather than the metric
that happens to favour our production choice, consistent with our
treatment of the analogous \texttt{proxy\_score} bias in Stage~E1.

Mean IoU increases monotonically with $K$ across the entire tested
range, but the rate of increase drops sharply beyond $K=250$: the gain
from $K=250$ to $K=400$ ($+0.0046$) is smaller than the single step
from $K=200$ to $K=250$ ($+0.0129$), indicating a plateau beginning
around $K=250$--$300$. \texttt{orientation\_accuracy} remains stable
(0.923--0.928) across the entire range, showing no trade-off between
pair count and orientation quality --- unlike a trade-off suggested by
an earlier, incorrectly-configured version of this sweep, in which a
missing \texttt{--orientation} flag caused Stage~E2 to run with a
different orientation heuristic than production. We select $K=250$: it
falls just past the point of diminishing returns, avoiding both the
larger, saturating pair counts that only a handful of the largest
classes can still make use of (Sec.~\ref{sec:limitations}) and the
comparatively larger gains left on the table by the original $K=60$.

We validated $K=250$ end-to-end: running the full E1--E5 pipeline under
the icoseg38 protocol (38 classes, 643 images, honest ground-truth
evaluation, no oracle access at inference time), against the previous
production value $K=60$, both under identical $\tau=0.05$,
$\alpha=0.10$, and \texttt{spatial} orientation. The recalibrated
configuration improves macro accuracy from $92.53\%$ to $94.23\%$
($+1.70$ points) and macro IoU from $76.01\%$ to $77.34\%$ ($+1.33$
points), and reduces the number of images requiring oracle
foreground/background correction from 23 to 8 (out of 643) --- a
reduction in orientation instability that the Stage~E2 diagnostic alone
did not predict, since \texttt{orientation\_accuracy} measured in
isolation is stable across $K$ (Table~\ref{tab:e2-topk-sweep}). $K=250$
is adopted as the new production default.

We additionally tested the degree-reweighting strength $\alpha \in
\{0.25, 0.50\}$ against the calibrated $\alpha=0.10$, end-to-end, under
the recalibrated $K=250$: $\alpha=0.25$ is approximately neutral (a
small accuracy gain paired with a small IoU loss) and $\alpha=0.50$
degrades both metrics. This does not reproduce a pattern suggested by
earlier, differently-configured runs, and $\alpha=0.10$ is retained
(Sec.~\ref{sec:limitations}, \emph{Rejected alternatives}).

Table~\ref{tab:e2-clamp} quantifies how often the calibrated bound
$K=250$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:e2}) is actually binding across the 38 iCoseg
classes used for evaluation ($N$ ranging from 4 to 41, mean $16.9$,

enough that $\binom{N}{2} \leq K$, so every possible pair is processed
and the top-$K$ selection has no effect; genuine truncation only
occurs for the $10$ largest classes, listed in

functions primarily as a safety bound against the small number of
large, densely-populated classes in the collection, rather than as an
active pruning mechanism applied uniformly across the dataset.

Recurring-background suppression (Stage~E2B, $\lambda=0.50$) was
independently re-validated end-to-end under $K=250$: disabling it
lowers macro accuracy from $94.23\%$ to $92.32\%$ and macro IoU from
$77.34\%$ to $72.75\%$ across all 38 classes, confirming the production
default. Its effect is not uniform, however: on a targeted subset of
classes with especially strong recurring backgrounds, several
suppression variants underperformed disabling it entirely
(\texttt{ablation/e2b/}), a heterogeneity we do not further resolve
here.

\paragraph{Mutual top-$k$ edge weight duplication.}
The global graph construction of Sec.~\ref{sec:e3-graph} inserts an edge from both endpoints' neighbor lists, so a pair that is mutually top-$k$ -- each patch appears among the other's $k$ nearest neighbors -- is inserted twice and its weight is summed, effectively doubling its contribution to the affinity matrix relative to a one-directional pair. To test whether this is consequential, a deduplicated variant (each edge inserted at most once) was run on the six calibration classes under the production configuration ($n_{\text{clusters}}=8$, $k=15$, $\alpha=0.5$, \texttt{fg\_decision\_mode=saliency}), holding every other stage fixed.

Liverpool's low score reflects an intrinsic property of the class rather

all 54 configurations by their worst-performing class (minimax), the
production configuration ties for rank~3, within the same
statistically-indistinguishable top group identified by the mean criterion
above. The dual criterion is therefore satisfied on both axes.

\subsection{Stage~E3 Cluster Selection: Failure Mode Decomposition}
\label{sec:ablation-e3-selection}

To understand where Stage~E3's cluster-selection rule
(\texttt{fg\_decision\_mode=saliency}) underperforms, we decompose its
gap to an oracle upper bound across all 38 iCoseg classes of the honest
baseline (\texttt{icoseg38\_topk250}). For each class we compute an
oracle that selects the best union of up to three global clusters against
ground truth (never used at decision time), both as a single class-wide
choice and per-image, following the same GT-for-diagnosis-only protocol
as Sec.~\ref{sec:e1-diagnostics}. Classes are labelled \emph{selection
failure} when a good global cluster choice exists but the production rule
does not find it, \emph{cluster consistency failure} when the best cluster
identity varies across images within a class, and \emph{clustering
failure} when no cluster union recovers the object even under oracle
selection.

Of the 38 classes, 26 (68.4\%) are \emph{e3\_reasonable} --- the production
selection is already close to the oracle global optimum --- and 7 (18.4\%)
are intermediate. Only 3 classes (7.9\%) show a clear selection failure
(Agra Taj Mahal--Inde du Nord, Airshows--helicopter, Gymnastics-1; gaps of
$0.21$--$0.31$ IoU to the oracle), and 2 (5.3\%) show a genuine clustering
failure --- both Airshows subcollections, where even the oracle cannot
recover more than $0.13$--$0.17$ IoU, consistent with the small-object,
low-contrast granularity bottleneck identified in
Sec.~\ref{sec:e1-diagnostics}. Averaged across all 38 classes, the gap
between the production selection and the global oracle is small
($+0.008$ mean IoU), while the gap between the per-image and global
oracle is larger ($+0.038$), quantifying the ceiling recoverable by
cluster-consistency failures specifically.

We additionally tested 11 unsupervised alternative selection rules ---
ranking clusters by raw E2B score, by final E3 score, by combinations
with spatial and border priors, and single- vs.\ two-cluster union
variants --- none using ground truth. None improves on the production
rule's mean IoU ($0.559$); several degrade it substantially, down to
$0.185$ for the single best-E2B-cluster rule ($-67\%$ relative). This
extends the single rejected alternative already reported for
\texttt{fg\_decision\_mode} to a broader negative result: the production
selection rule is not an arbitrary choice among untested alternatives.

\subsection{Stage~E3 Cluster Selection: Geometric Priors (Rejected)}
\label{sec:ablation-e3-geoprior}

Motivated by classes where the background is more consistent across images
than the object itself (e.g.\ Liverpool, where the pitch is stable while
players vary in pose, position, and count), we tested three geometric
priors as candidate replacements or complements to the production
cluster-to-foreground mapping (\texttt{fg\_decision\_mode=saliency}):
a \emph{border} prior penalising clusters touching the true image border,
an \emph{extent} prior penalising clusters with large bounding-box
coverage, and a \emph{position} prior penalising clusters whose centroid
is stable across images. None of the three priors, nor their combination,
uses ground truth in the decision; GT is used only to score the outcome,
following the same protocol as
Sec.~\ref{sec:ablation-e3-selection}.

Tested individually at the production decision threshold
($\text{fg\_threshold}=0.5$) across the 14 calibration classes, all three
priors underperform the production rule by a wide margin (mean IoU
$0.26$/$0.25$/$0.05$ for border/extent/position, respectively, against
$0.58$ for production), confirming that the E2/E2B signal already
dominates and no single geometric prior carries enough signal on its own.
The best combined candidate, an equal-weight blend of the production score
and all three geometric priors, does improve the two classes that
motivated this experiment --- Liverpool ($+0.028$) and Red Sox Players
($+0.036$) --- but degrades several others, most severely Alaskan Brown
Bear ($-0.145$, a larger absolute drop than any hyperparameter change
tested in this paper) along with Stonehenge ($-0.078$), Elephant
($-0.072$), and Panda ($-0.051$); mean IoU across the 14 classes falls
from $0.580$ to $0.560$. This fails our dual criterion
(Sec.~\ref{sec:ablation-setup}) on both axes --- mean and worst-case --- and
none of the tested weightings between the production score and the
geometric priors recovers both. We therefore retain
\texttt{fg\_decision\_mode=saliency} without geometric priors, consistent
with our treatment of the analogous rejected alternatives in
Secs.~\ref{sec:ablation-e2} and~\ref{sec:ablation-e3-selection}.

box+points+mask consistently outperforms box-only and box+points

winning configuration is not an artifact of overfitting to specific
classes within the calibration set. We recompute the winning
configuration 14 times via leave-one-class-out (LOCO) re-selection:
for each class, the configuration is chosen using the other 13
classes only, excluding the held-out class from its own selection,
and then evaluated on that held-out class under the config chosen
without it. The box+points+mask / 2-iteration / Hiera-Large

participate jointly in the selection, $\Delta = 0.0000$), indicating
the choice is stable to the composition of the calibration set rather
than driven by any single class's idiosyncrasies. This check bounds
sensitivity to \emph{which} classes are included in calibration; it
does not by itself establish that performance on \texttt{icoseg38}
classes entirely outside the 14-class calibration set matches the
reported $0.741$.

We fix the upstream configuration (feature extraction and spectral
co-segmentation) to the best global setting found in
Sec.~\ref{sec:ablation-e3} (DINOv2 ViT-S/14, $n_c{=}8$, $k{=}40$,
$\tau_{fg}{=}0.5$, $\alpha{=}0.5$) and sweep the SAM~2 refinement
stage across prompt strategy, number of refinement iterations, and
model size (SAM~2.1 Hiera Tiny/Small/Base+/Large), evaluating all
$3\times2\times4=24$ combinations on the 14 iCoseg classes with
available ground truth (336 configuration-class runs, full GT
coverage in every configuration).

Without refinement, mean pixel IoU is $0.614 \pm 0.145$. The best
configuration (box+points+mask prompting, 2 iterations, Hiera-Large)
reaches $0.741 \pm 0.135$, a gain of $+0.128 \pm 0.080$
(Table~\ref{tab:ablation-e4}), improving 302 of 309 evaluated images
(97.7\%) and degrading only 7.

Three findings stand out. First, prompt richness dominates:
box+points+mask consistently outperforms box-only and box+points
across every model size and iteration count — point prompts alone,
without the coarse E3 mask, can even \emph{underperform} the
unrefined baseline (e.g.\ $-0.001$ for Hiera-Large, box+points,
1 iteration). Second, model capacity shows a non-monotonic effect:
within \{Tiny, Small, Base+\}, larger models bring no consistent
IoU improvement (Small and Base+ tie at $0.726$), and Tiny is
unstable across classes (gain std.\ exceeding the mean gain in
several configurations). Only the larger architectural jump to
Hiera-Large yields a robust, broad-based improvement — gains in 11
of 14 classes, not driven by a single outlier. Third, iteration
count trades robustness for magnitude: box+points+mask at
1 iteration improves nearly every image (99.0--99.7\% depending on
model) with smaller average gains, while 2 iterations improve fewer
images (92--98\%) but by a larger margin — consistent with the
first refinement pass making conservative corrections and the
second making larger, occasionally overcorrecting adjustments.

\subsection{Iterative Refinement Ablation (E5)}
\label{sec:ablation-e5}

Starting from the best E4 configuration (box+points+mask, 2 SAM
iterations, Hiera-Large; mean IoU $0.741 \pm 0.140$), we ablate the
iterative refinement stage over the number of foreground/background
point seeds resampled per iteration ($n_{fg}, n_{bg} \in
\{3,5,8\}$), running each configuration to convergence
(max.\ 30 iterations, IoU-based convergence threshold of $0.99$) and
extracting the full per-iteration IoU curve from a single execution
per configuration, evaluated on all 14 iCoseg classes with ground
truth (126 configuration-class runs, 100\% convergence, no run
truncated by the iteration ceiling).

The best configuration ($n_{fg}{=}5, n_{bg}{=}8$) reaches a mean
IoU of $0.854 \pm 0.096$, a further gain of $+0.113 \pm 0.104$ over
the E4 baseline (Table~\ref{tab:ablation-e5}; second-best,
$n_{fg}{=}8, n_{bg}{=}8$, is statistically indistinguishable at
$0.854$). Point-count choice has a modest but consistent effect:
sparser seeding on both sides ($3,3$) yields the lowest gain
($+0.097$), while richer background seeding in particular improves
results, and configurations converge in 5--7 iterations on average.

The gain from iterative refinement is strongly anti-correlated with
the E4 baseline IoU per class ($r=-0.74$): classes where E4 already
performed well see negligible further improvement (Panda,
$+0.001$; Brown Bear, $+0.009$), while classes where E4 struggled
see substantial recovery (Balloon, $0.544\to0.837$, $+0.293$;
Statue, $0.666\to0.957$, $+0.291$; Taj Mahal, $0.559\to0.807$,
$+0.248$). This indicates the iterative stage acts primarily as a
corrective mechanism for hard cases rather than a uniform boost,
complementing E4 rather than duplicating its effect.

As a final validation step, we tested whether the E3 winning configuration
found via the calibration sweep ($k{=}40$, Sec.~\ref{sec:ablation-e3}, obtained
under the \texttt{semantic} protocol with 14 merged superclasses) generalizes
to the full \texttt{icoseg38} protocol (38 original folders, no subgroup
fusion). It does not: aggregate accuracy decreased ($0.9423\to0.9391$) and
IoU remained statistically flat ($0.7734\to0.7741$), masking catastrophic
per-class degradation — Gymnastics-1 lost 28.3 points of IoU
($0.446\to0.163$), Skating-ISU Grand Prix lost 20.5 points, and 8 further
classes lost 2--9 points. Notably, other sub-splits of the same nominal
class were unaffected or improved (Gymnastics-2, $+3.3$pp; Gymnastics-3,
$+0.0$pp), indicating the failure is tied to per-group image count rather
than semantic content: $k{=}40$ was calibrated on the \texttt{semantic}
protocol, where classes like Gymnastics are fused into a single
higher-cardinality superclass, and does not transfer to the smaller,
un-fused sub-splits present in \texttt{icoseg38}. This result reinforces
our rejection of per-class hyperparameter overrides
(Sec.~\ref{sec:ablation-methodology}) and extends it: generalization
failures can arise not only across semantic categories but across protocol
granularity for the same underlying hyperparameter. We therefore retain
$k{=}15$ (the previously validated production value) rather than adopt the
\texttt{semantic}-calibrated winner.
